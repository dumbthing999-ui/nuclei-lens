## Visible patterns

The gallery includes many AI-branded assistants and dashboards: agents, student planning/admissions, financial/news analysis, civic-scheme matching, document explanation, club analytics, and security/fraud detection. Several make broad claims while the listing alone cannot show their underlying method or evidence. This is a visible pattern, not a judgment that each individual project is shallow.

Impact areas already represented include flood intelligence, water quality, health supply allocation, medical exercise feedback, emergency alert translation, agriculture, recycling, renewable energy, women's safety, food portions, and fraud protection. A concept in these spaces needs a distinct mechanism and a measured edge, not a new dashboard skin.

## Strongest evidence bars observed (not a placement prediction)

- **Qayğısız** is the strongest directly inspected engineering/evaluation example: on-device rule-based SMS scoring, a fixed held-out split over 4,538 Azerbaijani messages, a reported 90.3% scam catch rate and 1.9% false-alarm rate, repeatable split indices, explicit failure analysis, and an honestly constrained demo. Its own page discloses translated data and no month-long real-traffic validation. It demonstrates how measured insight plus candor can outweigh AI branding.
- **Misconception Lab** frames student errors as competing, testable hypotheses and selects diagnostic follow-up questions using an inspectable deterministic engine. It provides a public demo and test-oriented implementation. Its real classroom learning benefit is not established by the inspected page.
- **FloodSense** discloses its input feeds and heuristic composite score, including a missing-river-data fallback, with a live demo link. It does not establish calibration against observed flood outcomes in the inspected material; a new flood-score dashboard would now be directly duplicative.
- **AquaGuard AI** uses explicit water-parameter thresholds and WHO guideline references. The inspected material does not establish clinical/pathogen prediction validity; claims that chemistry/clarity alone identifies community pathogen risk need careful scrutiny.
- **Deictic** has a crisp deterministic caption-to-equation insight, inspectable candidate ranking, and a baseline on authored synthetic fixtures. Its project page explicitly states that it predates the hackathon, so it is not evidence of a new EurekaDev-built project and is subject to the stated prior-work deduction.
- **AETHER** describes a technically interesting luminescent solar-concentrator concept and identifies durability/photodegradation as a real material challenge. Measured prototype output was not established in the inspected section.
- **SwasthSetu** and **NewsBridge** target meaningful systems problems with explicit feeds/workflows, but operational data access, measured model/translation performance, and reliability need verification before using similar claims.

## Saturation and weak spots

1. **AI assistant / dashboard pitches** are common. A prompt, a similarity score, a set of tabs, or several agents is not a defensible core insight by itself.
2. **Student planning and study assistance** is well represented, including a sophisticated deterministic misconception-diagnosis example. A generic tutor or planner is a poor bet.
3. **Scam/fraud detection** has multiple entries, including Qayğısız's strong reproducible evidence. Avoid the category unless the data, user intervention, and evaluated improvement are clearly different.
4. **Environment and public-safety tools** are present across flood, water, agriculture, recycling, heat/emergency communication, and safety. Their evidence gaps are not an invitation to copy them; they show that claims need validation and safety boundaries.
5. Many listing cards are too short to judge technical depth. The most common gallery-level weakness is **lack of visible proof at the pitch level**, not proven lack of implementation.

## White-space directions to test (not selected concepts)

- Public infrastructure accessibility or allocation with an explicit optimization objective, auditable inputs, a credible baseline, and a visible uncertainty boundary.
- Offline/local measurement or signal-processing tools whose value can be demonstrated on a fixed public dataset without depending on a paid inference API.
- Scientific decision tools that expose how a conclusion changes when measurements are missing or uncertain, evaluated against a simple baseline.
- Non-chatbot STEM interaction that links evidence to a measurable action; avoid cloning Deictic or Misconception Lab's specific educational mechanisms.

These directions are hypotheses. They must pass data/license/access checks, be distinct from the full gallery, and support a reliable 20–40 second demo before selection.
