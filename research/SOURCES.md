## Microscopy counting candidate (provisional)

21. Broad Bioimage Benchmark Collection, BBBC039 v1, retrieved 2026-10-07: https://bbbc.broadinstitute.org/BBBC039 — official dataset page documents 200 16-bit TIFF fields (520×696), about 23,000 manually annotated nuclei, separately downloadable images/masks/metadata, recommended train/validation/test partitions, and CC0. Primary dataset source; do not split by individual nuclei because images are the independent units.
22. Broad Bioimage Benchmark Collection, Image Sets catalogue, retrieved 2026-10-07: https://bbbc.broadinstitute.org/image_sets — lists BBBC039 as 200 fields of fluorescent U2OS nuclei images. Catalogue metadata is not a substitute for the dataset-specific annotation/mask documentation.
23. “An Integrative Segmentation Framework for Cell Nucleus of Fluorescence Microscopy,” Genes 13(3), 431 (2022): https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4425/13/3/431 — reports about 23,165 expert-biologist annotations and describes a 100/50/50 split. Use the current official metadata package rather than assuming this paper's exact split.
24. “Quantitative benchmarking of nuclear segmentation algorithms in multiplexed immunofluorescence imaging for translational studies,” 2025 paper: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12125308/ — compares established pretrained models and tools including Cellpose, StarDist, Mesmer, Fiji, CellProfiler, and QuPath; demonstrates that ordinary segmentation is a mature and heavily benchmarked space. Dataset/task is not identical to BBBC039; do not transfer its results to this candidate.
25. ImageJ Foci Analyzer documentation, retrieved 2026-10-07: https://imagej.net/plugins/foci-analyzer — documents existing StarDist, Cellpose, and classical nuclei segmentation workflows and manual editing/removal. This is product documentation, not independent comparative validation.
26. Upschulte et al., “Uncertainty-Aware Contour Proposal Networks for Instance Segmentation of Cells and Nuclei in Microscopy Images,” Proceedings of Machine Learning Research 2023: https://proceedings.mlr.press/v212/upschulte23a.html — establishes that uncertainty-aware microscopy instance segmentation is already an active research direction. A perturbation-consensus/review-map concept cannot claim novelty merely by exposing uncertainty; differentiation requires a specific, experimentally validated workflow contribution.
27. Broad Institute, “Broad Bioimage Benchmark Collection” overview, retrieved 2026-10-07: https://bbbc.broadinstitute.org/bbbc/ — says the collection contains freely downloadable microscopy image sets and is intended for comparing methods. Dataset attribution and exact usage terms still need confirmation from the chosen set’s record.
28. “As Easy as 1, 2… 4? Uncertainty in Counting Tasks for Medical Imaging,” 2020: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7611043/ — directly studies uncertainty intervals for medical image counts, including cell counts, comparing uncertainty methods and a multi-task model. This is a direct novelty threat to a generic count-uncertainty product.
29. “Give me (un)certainty — An exploration of parameters that affect segmentation uncertainty,” arXiv:1911.06357 (2019): https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.06357 — presents uncertainty as a way to flag low-quality segmentations for further human review. This directly undermines a claim that uncertainty heatmaps plus review suggestions alone are novel.
30. “Assessing Reliability and Challenges of Uncertainty Estimations for Medical Image Segmentation,” arXiv:1907.03338 (2019): https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.03338 — studies reliability and challenges in segmentation uncertainty estimation; broad medical imaging scope, not an evaluation specific to BBBC039.
31. “Shortcomings and areas for improvement in digital pathology image segmentation challenges,” 2022: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36525770/ — review identifies reference annotation disagreement and evaluation-quality issues in segmentation challenges. It supports careful validation methodology, not demand for this proposed tool.

## Heat/cooling allocation novelty check

32. Adams et al., “Examining the Optimal Placement of Cooling Centers to Serve Populations at High Risk of Extreme Heat Exposure in 81 US Cities,” Public Health Reports (2023): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10576472/ — analyzes 1,402 cooling centers across 81 US cities and publishes an ArcGIS StoryMap template. It documents access disparities, but does not establish that any new app improves outcomes.
33. Watkins et al., “A Co-Produced Workflow for Addressing Inequities in Cooling Center Access,” Community Science (2024): https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2023CSJ000038 — presents a public-data optimization workflow co-developed for Phoenix/Tucson stakeholders, using location-allocation to select additional cooling centers. Directly overlaps a generic cooling-center allocation app.
34. “City-Heat Equity Adaptation Tool (City-HEAT),” Environmental Modelling & Software (2023): https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/57567 — multi-objective heat adaptation investment planning under uncertainty, including cooling centers, tested in Baltimore; code is publicly referenced. Direct novelty threat to generic robust heat-resource optimization.
35. World Resources Institute, “Cool Cities Lab,” published 2026-03-17, retrieved 2026-10-07: https://www.wri.org/data/cool-cities-lab — current open-data tool for identifying urban heat risks and planning interventions such as tree planting and cool roofs. Product overview, not independent effectiveness evaluation.

## NucleiLens decision update — retrieved 2026-10-08

36. Caicedo et al., “Evaluation of Deep Learning Strategies for Nucleus Segmentation in Fluorescence Images,” Cytometry Part A (2019): https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/cyto.a.23863 ; open manuscript https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6771982/ — evaluates the dataset associated with BBBC039 and analyzes merged/split objects; explains that pixel-overlap scores do not adequately capture relevant instance errors and recommends object-level evaluation. This establishes the task/mechanism motivation; it does not establish the proposed audit's superiority.
37. Gupta et al., “Topology-Aware Uncertainty for Image Segmentation,” 2024: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11526043/ — studies uncertainty at the level of curvilinear structures, using topology and graphs to identify structures for expert review. Direct prior art for structure/graph-based uncertainty; the proposed nuclei count-review workflow cannot claim global firstness for this broader idea.

## Research constraints

- No numerical impact claim for a proposed project is accepted solely because a source identifies the problem; project-level effect needs its own measured evaluation.
- IMD dashboards and advisories are changing operational data, not necessarily an openly downloadable, stable dataset. Data access, terms, and historical coverage must be proven before selecting an IMD-dependent concept.
- The 2019 IMD report is historic evidence and should not establish current national deployment counts.
